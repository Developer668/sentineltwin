# SentinelTwin design context

## Users

Emergency-operations planners are the primary users. Hackathon judges and technical evaluators are the secondary audience. Users need to assess a risk zone, inspect evidence provenance, run a memory-grounded scenario, and understand exactly what was or was not persisted.

## Product character

Calm, tactical, trustworthy, and information-dense. The interface should feel like a modern emergency coordination surface rather than a consumer SaaS dashboard. Status must be readable under pressure and claims must remain evidence-backed.

## Visual direction

Preserve the accepted industrial command-center composition: dark terrain, square hairline panels, compact condensed labels, orange for fire/action, violet for seismic, lime for verified/complete, and cyan for imagery/evidence. The map remains the visual anchor. Favor crisp hierarchy and restrained motion over decoration.

## Interaction priorities

1. Select a risk zone and hazard layer.
2. Run a simulation or scan/import imagery.
3. Read provenance, persistence, and human-review state.
4. Inspect shared-memory and resilience evidence without inferring unverified cloud behavior.

## Avoid

Consumer card grids, large decorative headlines, glassmorphism, gradient text, playful copy, invented real-time claims, or any visual treatment that competes with risk evidence. Do not imply actual failover, durable persistence, satellite analysis, or operational readiness without corresponding API evidence.

## Accessibility and constraints

Support keyboard-only operation, reduced motion, 320px minimum width, high-density desktop use, and a stacked mobile command feed. Maintain AA-level contrast for functional text and visible focus on every interactive control.
